## Legal Issues Raised

## Court's Analysis
The court reasoned that the right to education is a fundamental right of children, and private schools have a social obligation to contribute to its realization. The court considered the benefits received by private schools from the state, including land at concessional rates and tax exemptions, and held that these benefits imply a public obligation. The court also considered the exemption of minority educational institutions from this provision. The court's analysis balanced the rights of private schools with the need to ensure access to education for disadvantaged groups.

## Ruling / Holding
The court upheld the constitutional validity of Section 12(1)(c) of the Right to Education Act 2009, holding that the 25% reservation for economically weaker sections is a reasonable restriction. The court also clarified that the government must reimburse private schools for the expenditure incurred on students from weaker sections.

* The right to education is a fundamental right of children that implies a social obligation on private schools.
* Private schools that receive state benefits cannot claim absolute immunity from public obligations.
* The state can impose reasonable restrictions on private schools to ensure access to education for disadvantaged groups.
* Minority educational institutions are exempt from certain provisions related to reservation of seats.
