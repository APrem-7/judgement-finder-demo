## Summary of Facts
The petitioner, Person B Doe, challenged the validity of a provision in the Right to Education Act 2009, which requires private unaided non-minority schools to reserve seats for children from economically weaker sections. The petitioner argued that this provision infringes upon their rights as an educational institution. The case involved the petitioner and the government, with the former seeking to overturn the provision. The petitioner's challenge was heard by a court, which ultimately upheld the constitutional validity of the provision.

## Legal Issues Raised
* Whether Section 12(1)(c) of the Right to Education Act 2009 violates the fundamental right to carry on occupation under Article 19(1)(g)
* Whether private schools have a social obligation to contribute to the realization of the right to education under Article 21A
* Whether the 25% reservation for economically weaker sections is a reasonable restriction
* Whether minority educational institutions are exempt from this provision

## Court's Analysis
The court reasoned that the right to education is a fundamental right of children, and private schools, as part of society, have a social obligation to contribute to its realization. The court noted that private schools benefit from various state support, including land at concessional rates and tax exemptions, and therefore cannot claim absolute immunity from public obligations. The court also considered the exemption of minority educational institutions from this provision. The court's analysis balanced the rights of private schools with the social obligation to provide education to weaker sections of society.

## Ruling / Holding
The court upheld the constitutional validity of Section 12(1)(c) of the Right to Education Act 2009, holding that the 25% reservation for economically weaker sections is a reasonable restriction. The court also clarified that the government must reimburse private schools for the expenditure incurred on students from weaker sections.

## Key Legal Principles Established
* The right to education is a fundamental right of children, and private schools have a social obligation to contribute to its realization
* Private schools cannot claim absolute immunity from public obligations due to the state support they receive
* The 25% reservation for economically weaker sections is a reasonable restriction in the interest of weaker sections of society
* Minority educational institutions are exempt from the provision
