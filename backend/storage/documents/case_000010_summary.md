**Legal Issues Raised**  
- Whether the appellant can be convicted under **Section 498‑A IPC** for cruelty (physical and mental torture) inflicted on the spouse.  
- Whether the appellant can be convicted under **Section 306 IPC** for abetting the suicide of the spouse.  
- Whether the trial‑court’s conviction and the sentence imposed on the appellant are legally sustainable.

**Court’s Analysis**  
The court examined the prosecution’s evidence—including witness testimony describing the appellant’s abusive conduct and the circumstances of the deceased’s death—and the defence’s limited evidence. It noted that the appellant’s repeated attempts to force a second marriage, coupled with documented physical and mental abuse, satisfied the elements of cruelty under Section 498‑A. The court also considered the appellant’s alleged role in provoking the deceased to take her own life, meeting the criteria for abetment under Section 306. The appellate court reviewed the trial‑court’s findings and the High Court’s affirmation, finding no error in the application of law or assessment of facts, and therefore upheld the conviction and sentence.

**Ruling / Holding**  
The appellate court upheld the conviction of Person A under Sections 498‑A and 306 of the Indian Penal Code and affirmed the rigorous imprisonment sentences (three years for 498‑A and eight years for 306, to run concurrently) together with the fine of Rs 1,000.

**Key Legal Principles Established**  
- Section 498‑A IPC applies where a husband subjects his wife to physical or mental cruelty, even where the cruelty is linked to an extra‑marital relationship.  
- Section 306 IPC can be invoked when the accused’s conduct is shown to have directly abetted the victim’s suicide.  
- Courts must carefully evaluate the totality of evidence on cruelty and abetment; mere allegations are insufficient without corroborative proof.  
- Appellate courts may affirm lower‑court convictions when the legal reasoning and factual findings are sound and uncontradicted by the record.
