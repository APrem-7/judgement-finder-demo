## Summary of Facts  
The appellant, a real‑estate developer, challenged an order of the consumer forum that permitted a group of 91 purchasers of 51 apartments in a residential complex of 1,134 units to file a consumer complaint in a representative capacity on behalf of all purchasers. The respondents, those 91 buyers, sought compensation for delay and other reliefs under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019. The builder contended that the small number of plaintiffs and the lack of a common grievance precluded a class‑action filing, while the respondents argued that all purchasers shared the same interest and that precedent supported the forum’s discretion to allow the representative suit.

## Legal Issues Raised  
- Whether a complaint filed by a minority of purchasers (91 out of 1,134) can be entertained as a representative action under Section 35(1)(c) of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.  
- Whether the requirement of “common interest” or “sameness of interest” is satisfied in the present facts.  
- Whether the consumer forum correctly exercised its discretion under Order I Rule 8 of the Code of Civil Procedure in granting permission for a class‑action suit.  
- Whether the forum’s decision is consistent with earlier Supreme Court judgments on representative consumer actions.

## Court's Analysis  
The Court first examined the statutory language of Section 35(1)(c), emphasizing that the provision is intended to enable a small group of aggrieved consumers to represent the larger class when the interests are identical. It then applied the “sameness of interest” test, noting that all purchasers contracted with the same developer for identical units in the same project and were subject to the same promised delivery dates and delay penalties. The Court observed that prior Supreme Court decisions (e.g., Chairman, [ORGANIZATION] Person A vs Person B and Vikrant [ORGANIZATION] vs [ORGANIZATION]) had affirmed that the numerical minority of plaintiffs does not defeat a representative suit if the requisite commonality exists. Accordingly, the Court held that the forum’s reliance on these precedents was appropriate and that the builder’s argument of “miniscule percentage” was untenable. Finally, the Court affirmed the forum’s discretion under Order I Rule 8, which permits the court to consolidate similar claims for efficient adjudication.

## Ruling / Holding  
The appeal is dismissed; the consumer forum’s order allowing the representative complaint under Section 35(1)(c) of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 is upheld.

## Key Legal Principles Established  
- A representative consumer action may be instituted by a minority of affected consumers provided there is a common grievance affecting the entire class.  
- The “sameness of interest” test focuses on the nature of the contract and the uniformity of the alleged breach, not on the proportion of plaintiffs to total consumers.  
- The discretion of the consumer forum under Order I Rule 8 of the CPC to permit class actions is affirmed when statutory criteria are satisfied.  
- Earlier Supreme Court rulings on representative consumer suits remain binding guidance for lower tribunals.
