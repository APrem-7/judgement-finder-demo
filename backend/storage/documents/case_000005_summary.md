## Summary of Facts  
Person G was arrested on 30 November 1998 together with several co‑accused for alleged offences under Sections 302, 149, 325 read with 149 and 323/149 of the Indian Penal Code. The Additional Sessions Judge initially held that Person G was below 16 years of age at the time of the offence and ordered that he be tried under the Juvenile Justice Act, 2000. On revision, the State (Respondent No. 2) obtained a declaration that Person G was above 16 years and therefore the provisions of the Juvenile Justice Act, 2000 were inapplicable. The appellant, Person G, challenges that finding, contending that the Act should protect persons who have not completed 18 years of age.

## Legal Issues Raised  
- Whether the appellant’s age at the time of the alleged offence falls within the definition of “juvenile” under the Juvenile Justice Act, 2000.  
- Whether the amendment raising the juvenile age limit from 16 to 18 years applies prospectively to offences committed before the amendment.  
- What evidentiary standards govern the determination of age, specifically the admissibility and reliability of school registers and medical reports under Section 35 of the Indian Evidence Act, 1872.  
- Whether the High Court’s technical approach defeats the rehabilitative purpose of the Juvenile Justice Act, 2000.  

## Court's Analysis  
The Court examined the appellant’s claimed date of birth (17 October 1982) and the medical examination report, both indicating an age of between 16 and 17 years at the time of the incident. It reviewed precedent on age determination, noting that school‑register entries made ante litem are admissible as reliable evidence under Section 35 of the Evidence Act. The Court also considered the legislative intent behind the Juvenile Justice Act, 2000, which was to expand protection to persons who have not completed 18 years of age. However, the Court held that the statutory language of the 2000 Act, as interpreted in earlier judgments, applies only to persons who were below 16 years on the date of the offence unless a specific prospective clause is present, which was absent in this case.

## Ruling / Holding  
The Court affirmed the revision order, holding that the appellant was not a juvenile within the meaning of the Juvenile Justice Act, 2000, and consequently the Act’s special procedures do not apply to him.

## Key Legal Principles Established  
- The age of a juvenile must be ascertained on the exact date of the alleged offence, not on the date of trial or amendment of the statute.  
- School‑register entries and contemporaneous medical reports are admissible evidence for age determination under Section 35 of the Indian Evidence Act, 1872.  
- Legislative amendments that raise the juvenile age limit are not applied retrospectively unless expressly stated.  
- The rehabilitative objective of the Juvenile Justice Act, 2000 does not override clear statutory age thresholds.
