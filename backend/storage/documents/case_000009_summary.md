## Summary of Facts  
The appellant (Person A, a police constable) died on 11 February 1997 near a shop in Dharampur while standing behind a bus (registration HP‑14‑3596) owned by the first respondent, Himachal Road Transport Corpn. The bus driver (Person B) and the conductor (Om Dutt) were alleged to have reversed the vehicle without sounding a horn or giving any warning, causing Person A’s death. The respondents denied that an accident occurred, contending that the deceased had died the previous evening and that the body was later found wrapped in a blanket at some distance from the bus. The dispute gave rise to a claim for compensation under Section 166 of the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988.

## Legal Issues Raised  
- Whether the death of Person A was caused by the rash and negligent reversal of the bus by Person B and the negligent conduct of the conductor, Om Dutt.  
- Whether the respondents can be held liable for compensation under Section 166 of the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988.  
- Whether the evidence (post‑mortem report, eyewitness testimony, and statements of the driver and conductor) is sufficient to establish negligence and causation.  

## Court's Analysis  
The Tribunal examined the First Information Report, the post‑mortem report (exhibit PY) showing brain injury as the cause of death, and the testimony of an eye‑witness (Shri Dharam Pal). It noted that the driver and conductor admitted being present at the scene, and that a witness (Vijay Jane Doe) claimed to have seen a body wrapped in a blanket behind the bus before it started moving. Although the reliability of some statements was questioned, the Tribunal found the cumulative evidence credible enough to infer that the bus was reversed without any warning and that Person A was struck. Consequently, it concluded that the respondents were negligent. On appeal, the higher court scrutinised the post‑mortem findings, observing that there were no tyre‑mark injuries and that the principal injury was to the head, leading it to doubt the claim that the body was crushed by the bus. This inconsistency prompted the reversal of the Tribunal’s finding.  

## Ruling / Holding  
The Tribunal originally held the respondents liable for negligence and ordered compensation under Section 166. The appellate court set aside that finding, concluding that the evidence did not conclusively prove that the bus caused the fatal injuries.  

## Key Legal Principles Established  
- Liability under Section 166 of the Motor Vehicles Act requires a clear causal link between the vehicle’s negligent act and the victim’s death.  
- Post‑mortem reports must substantiate the nature of injuries alleged to have resulted from the vehicle’s impact.  
- Eyewitness testimony, while persuasive, must be corroborated by forensic evidence to establish negligence conclusively.  
- A denial of the occurrence of an accident by the respondents does not, by itself, preclude liability if the factual matrix supports the claim.  

## Statutes and Provisions Discussed  
- Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 – Section 166 (Compensation for death or injury caused by a motor vehicle).
