## Summary of Facts  
Person G, a daily‑wage worker, was engaged by Respondent No. 1 (the Haryana State Agriculture Marketing Board) on 1 September 1995 and worked until 18 July 1996. He was paid a consolidated salary of Rs 1,498 per month. After his services ceased, Person G contended that his termination violated Section 25F of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, and sought reinstatement with continuity of service and full back wages. The Industrial Tribunal held that he had rendered more than 240 days of service in the relevant year and that the respondent had failed to give notice, pay in lieu of notice, or retrenchment compensation, consequently awarding reinstatement and back wages. The High Court set aside that award, holding it unsustainable in law, and the matter was appealed before this Court by special leave.

## Legal Issues Raised  
- Whether a termination that contravenes Section 25F of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 automatically obliges the employer to reinstate the workman with continuity of service and full back wages.  
- Whether the appellate court may disturb a tribunal’s award of reinstatement and back wages when the termination is deemed illegal.  
- What factors, if any, justify granting compensation in lieu of reinstatement despite a finding of illegal retrenchment.  

## Court's Analysis  
The Court noted that earlier jurisprudence treated reinstatement with back wages as the usual remedy for illegal termination, but recent decisions have shifted away from a mechanical application of that rule. Citing the judgment in *Person D Ltd. v. Person E Pandey*, the Court emphasized that relief must be tailored to the facts and the broader purpose of the statute, rather than being a “cold letter of the law.” It observed that the Supreme Court, in *[ORGANIZATION] v. [LOCATION] Joshi*, reiterated that reinstatement is not automatic; the court must consider the suitability of reinstatement, the conduct of the parties, and the prevailing economic context. Accordingly, the Court held that while the termination was unlawful, the award of reinstatement with full back wages could be set aside if such relief would be inappropriate in the circumstances, and compensation may be a more just remedy.

## Ruling / Holding  
The Court upheld the High Court’s decision to set aside the tribunal’s award of reinstatement and full back wages, allowing the respondent to be relieved from the obligation to reinstate Person G.  

## Key Legal Principles Established  
- Relief of reinstatement with back wages is not automatic upon a finding of illegal termination under Section 25F; courts must exercise discretion based on the facts.  
- The purpose of the Industrial Disputes Act includes delivering substantive justice, which may favor compensation over reinstatement where appropriate.  
- Judicial discretion must consider policy changes such as market‑economy reforms, globalization, and privatization when determining appropriate relief.
