## Summary of Facts
The appellant, Person G, was engaged as a daily‑wage worker by Haryana State Agriculture Marketing Board (Respondent No. 1) on 1 September 1995 and worked until 18 July 1996, receiving a consolidated salary of Rs 1,498 per month. The appellant claimed that his termination was illegal because it violated Section 25F of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, and sought reinstatement with continuity of service and full back wages. The Presiding Officer held that the appellant had rendered more than 240 days of service in the relevant year and, finding a breach of Section 25F, awarded reinstatement with back wages effective from the date of the demand notice (27 January 1997).

## Legal Issues Raised
- Whether the appellant’s termination contravened Section 25F of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947.  
- Whether a breach of Section 25F automatically entitles the workman to reinstatement with continuity of service and full back wages.  
- Whether the High Court was justified in setting aside the award of the Presiding Officer on the ground that reinstatement and back wages were not appropriate.

## Court's Analysis
The Court examined the statutory framework of Section 25F and the jurisprudential evolution concerning relief for illegal retrenchment. While earlier decisions treated reinstatement with back wages as the usual remedy, recent judgments emphasize that such relief is not automatic; the Court must weigh factors such as the length of service, the passage of time, the employee’s age, and the practicality of reinstatement. The Court also considered the policy shift reflected in cases like *Person D Ltd. v. Person E Pandey* and *[ORGANIZATION] v. Joshi*, which underscore that compensation may be a more suitable remedy where reinstatement would be impracticable or inequitable.

## Ruling / Holding
The Supreme Court held that the High Court was not justified in overturning the award; the appellant was entitled to reinstatement with continuity of service and full back wages, although the Court reiterated that such relief is not automatic and must be assessed on the facts of each case.

## Key Legal Principles Established
- Relief of reinstatement with full back wages is discretionary, not automatic, even where termination breaches Section 25F.  
- Courts must apply a “mindful” approach, considering practical and equitable factors (e.g., elapsed time, employee’s age, feasibility of reinstatement).  
- Compensation in lieu of reinstatement may be ordered where reinstatement would not serve the ends of justice.  
- The statutory “cold letter” rule is tempered by the overarching purpose of the Industrial Disputes Act to achieve substantive justice.
